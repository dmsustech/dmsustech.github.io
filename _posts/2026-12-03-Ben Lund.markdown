How many spanned lines can we guarantee through a single point of a finite set? For n points affinely spanning ℂᵈ, we show that some point lies on at least n+1−2n/(d+1) spanned lines. This bound is attained for infinitely many n in each dimension.

By repeated contraction, this implies a sharp upper bound of r+(ℓ−3)r(r−1)/2 on the number of elements of a simple rank-r complex representable matroid with no U₂,ℓ minor, which extends a theorem of Geelen, Nelson, and Walsh to every rank. As a further application, a rank-r integer matrix whose maximal minors have absolute value at most Δ has O(Δr²) distinct columns.

I will sketch the proof of the line-degree bound, focusing on frame energy, which is the sum of the squared absolute inner products between distinct normalized point vectors. A suitable normalization provides a global upper bound, while collinear sets give local lower bounds.

This is joint work with Matthew Kroeker.
